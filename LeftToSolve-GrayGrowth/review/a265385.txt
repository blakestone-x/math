Source conjecture: (a(2n)/a(n))^(1/n) tends to 2; the adjacent ratios a(n)/a(n-1) show small persistent fluctuations around 2.

Statement scope:
The proposed theorem formalizes the real limit and a standard precise persistence assertion: there exists epsilon>0 such that both s(m)<2-epsilon and s(n)>2+epsilon occur at arbitrarily large indices. The approximate one-percent description in the source does not prescribe an exact numerical bound; the statement does not invent one.
The definitions of gray, a, and meanGrowth are identical to Blake Stone's existing user-math/A265385 proof. The source uses one-based indexing. The auxiliary a(0)=0 is never used for positive-index recurrence terms and does not affect either asymptotic assertion. ConsecutiveRatio n is exactly the source's a(n)/a(n-1).
The proof imports these exact definitions and does not define a replacement sequence.

Mathematical mechanism:
For every k>=0, the normalized prefix pair (floor(a(k+9)/2^k), floor(a(k+10)/2^(k+1))) is one of A=(211,209), B=(209,211), C=(211,211). The exact recurrence forces A->B, B->A or C, and C->A. Therefore A and B each occur in every three consecutive states.
At A, 211*a(k+10)<420*a(k+9); at B, 422*a(k+9)<210*a(k+10). Positivity translates these to real ratios below 420/211 and above 422/210. The explicit epsilon=1/106 satisfies both required separations from 2.
The main theorem has no hypotheses and conjoins the original limit with this persistence property.
