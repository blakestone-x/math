# An elementary recursion for Ducci preperiod counts

For even width, counting the tuples entering a cycle by step $h$ reduces to counting the kernel of multiplication by $t^h$ in

$$
R_{k,\ell}=(\mathbb Z/p^\ell\mathbb Z)[t]/((1+t)^{p^k}-1).
$$

Here $p$ is prime, $k\ge0$ and $\ell\ge1$. This note derives the count using finite rings and the known nilpotency index. The companion note explains the reduction from adjacent sums, treats general widths and moduli, and gives a second derivation from the lens-space computation of Mahammed and Yanagida. Write $F_{k,\ell}(h)=\log_p|\ker(t^h:R_{k,\ell}\to R_{k,\ell})|$. For $k\ge1$ set $d=p^{k-1}$ and $H(k,\ell)=p^k+(\ell-1)(p^k-d)$. The nilpotency index of $t$ is exactly $H(k,\ell)$. Set $H(0,\ell)=1$, since $t=0$ when $k=0$. [Clark and Schauz, §4.3, proof of Theorem 4.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.02763)

**Theorem.** For $k\ge1$ and $\ell\ge2$,

$$
F_{k,\ell}(h)=\min(h,H(k,\ell))+F_{k-1,\ell-1}(h),\qquad h\ge0.
$$

The boundary cases are $F_{k,1}(h)=\min(h,p^k)$ and $F_{0,\ell}(h)=\ell\min(h,1)$. Consequently, with $s=\min(k,\ell)$,

$$
F_{k,\ell}(h)=(\ell-s)\min(h,1)+
\sum_{i=1}^{s}\min\!\left(h,p^{k-i}\bigl[1+(p-1)(\ell-i+1)\bigr]\right).
$$

## The order of each successive quotient

Put $R=R_{k,\ell}$ and $e_{k,\ell}(j)=\log_p|t^jR/t^{j+1}R|$ for $j\ge0$. This quotient is additively cyclic, generated by the class of $t^j$. Its exponent in base $p$ is

$$
e_{k,\ell}(j)=
\min_{t^j V=0}\nu_\ell(V(0)),
$$

where $V(0)$ is the constant coefficient in $\mathbb Z/p^\ell\mathbb Z$ and $\nu_\ell(0)=\ell$. Indeed, $a t^j\in t^{j+1}R$ exactly when some annihilator of $t^j$ has constant coefficient $a$. The constant coefficient is well defined because the defining relation vanishes at zero. This also covers $j=0$, where the only annihilator is zero and $e_{k,\ell}(0)=\ell$.

Whenever $j<H(k,\ell)$, we have $e_{k,\ell}(j)\ge1$. A polynomial with constant coefficient prime to $p$ is a unit in $R$, since $t$ is nilpotent. Such a polynomial cannot annihilate $t^j\ne0$.

## Passing to a smaller cyclic group

Let $k\ge1$, $\ell\ge2$, $w=(p-1)d$ and $H'=H(k-1,\ell-1)$. We show that for $0\le j<H'$, every annihilator $V$ of $t^j$ maps to a multiple of $p$ in $R_{k-1,\ell}$.

Factor the defining relation of $R$ by using the monic degree-$w$ polynomial

$$
g(t)=\frac{(1+t)^{p^k}-1}{(1+t)^d-1}
=\sum_{v=0}^{p-1}(1+t)^{dv}.
$$

It has constant coefficient $p$ and $g(t)\equiv t^w\pmod p$. In the quotient $S=(\mathbb Z/p^\ell\mathbb Z)[t]/g$, write $\pi$ for the image of $t$. Then $\pi^{\ell w}=0$. Also $S/(\pi)\cong\mathbb F_p$, so every additive quotient $(\pi^i)/(\pi^{i+1})$ has at most $p$ elements. Since $g$ is monic, $|S|=p^{\ell w}$. All $\ell w$ successive quotients through $(\pi^{\ell w})=0$ therefore have exactly $p$ elements. Each nonzero element of $S$ is $\pi^i$ times a unit for its largest possible $i$, because any element outside $(\pi)$ is a unit. It follows that $\pi^j V(\pi)=0$ implies $V(\pi)\in(\pi^{\ell w-j})$ for $0\le j\le\ell w$.

The index $H'$ is at most $(\ell-1)d$. Indeed, the smaller ring $R_{k-1,\ell-1}$ has order $p^{(\ell-1)d}$ and each of its nonzero successive ideal inclusions down to zero is strict by nilpotence. Since $w\ge d$, for $j<H'$ we have $\ell w-j\ge d$. Hence $V(\pi)\in(\pi^d)$.

To interpret this, write $g(t)=t^w+pU(t)$, with $U(0)=1$. The element $U(\pi)$ is a unit, so $(p)=(\pi^w)$ in $S$. Since $d\le w$, reducing modulo $\pi^d$ gives $S/(\pi^d)\cong\mathbb F_p[t]/t^d$. This last ring is exactly $R_{k-1,1}$. Thus $V$ reduces to zero in $R_{k-1,1}$ and its image in $R_{k-1,\ell}$ equals $pG$ for some $G$.

The relation $t^j pG=0$ in $R_{k-1,\ell}$ gives $t^jG=0$ in $R_{k-1,\ell-1}$, since the smaller cyclic group ring has a monic defining relation and hence a free coefficient basis. Its constant coefficient satisfies $V(0)=pG(0)\pmod{p^\ell}$. Taking the minimum over annihilators gives

$$
e_{k,\ell}(j)\ge 1+e_{k-1,\ell-1}(j)\qquad(0\le j<H').
$$

## Equality by counting

Write $H=H(k,\ell)$. We have $H'\le H$, and the smaller ring's successive quotients vanish for $j\ge H'$. Together with $e_{k,\ell}(j)\ge1$ for $j<H$, the last inequality gives

$$
e_{k,\ell}(j)\ge \mathbf1_{j<H}+e_{k-1,\ell-1}(j)\qquad(j\ge0).
$$

Summing over $j$, the left side is $\log_p|R|=\ell p^k$. The right side sums to $H+(\ell-1)d=\ell p^k$. Hence equality holds for every $j$. Finally, the kernel and cokernel of an endomorphism of a finite abelian group have equal order. Thus $F_{k,\ell}(h)=\log_p|R/t^hR|=\sum_{0\le j<h}e_{k,\ell}(j)$, which proves the recursion, including $h=0$. The boundary formulas follow immediately from $R_{k,1}=\mathbb F_p[t]/t^{p^k}$ and $R_{0,\ell}=\mathbb Z/p^\ell\mathbb Z$ with $t=0$. Iterating proves the displayed closed form. $\square$
