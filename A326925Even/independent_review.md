## 1. Exact question and normalization

The complete official records A326925, A328610, and A328611 were independently fetched from `oeis/oeisdata` and read. Their precise revisions and blob hashes are documented in [the targeted source review](source_checks.md). The main target is the even-degree clause: each degree-$2k$ numerator polynomial has two irreducible factors of degree $k$, for every positive $k$.

The source title shifts derivative order relative to polynomial degree. The existing formal bridge proves

$$
f^{(n)}(x)=\frac{n!P_{n+1}(x)}{(1-x-x^2)^{n+1}},
\qquad f(x)=\frac{1-x}{1-x-x^2}.
$$

Thus the new degree-$2k$ assertion corresponds to odd derivative order $2k-1$. The source's displayed polynomials and odd-order factorization program identify this intended family. The new endpoint retains the exact range $k\ge1$, rather than silently interpreting natural subtraction at $k=0$.

The factor normalization is explicit: $U_1=X$, $V_1=2-X$, with the common recurrence in the README. The source A326925 displays their product at degree two. Its companion arrays instead begin with $X-2$ and $-X$, exchanging the two factors and both signs. The README correctly explains this first-row exception and does not assert equality with arbitrary `FactorList` ordering. The precise constant formulas are $U_k(0)=F_{k-1}$ and $V_k(0)=L_{k-1}$, including $k=1$.

## 2. Signed binomial criterion

`SignedKummer.irreducible_of_signed_nonpowers` is a sufficient criterion for positive exponents: for each prime $p\mid n$, neither $a$ nor $-a$ is a $p$th power. It does not state an equivalence or a new classification of binomial irreducibility.

The proof strengthens the invariant to $(b^p)^2\ne a^2$. Prime-factor induction and mathlib's irreducibility-of-composition theorem reduce the problem to a simple extension generated by $\alpha^p=a$. Its norm is $(-1)^p(-a)$, whose square is $a^2$. Therefore a forbidden squared-root equality over the extension descends by the norm to one over the base. Squaring correctly removes the sign even at $p=2$. The integrality and power-basis steps used to evaluate the norm are proved, not assumed. The zero exponent is excluded and the exponent-one base case is correctly handled.

The consumer uses the criterion on $\pm u$ over $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$ and on $\pm iu$ over $J=K(i)$. It does not apply the signed criterion directly to $-u^2$ at an even exponent, where one of the signed exclusions would fail.

## 3. Quadratic extensions and nonpower arguments

The signed square obstruction uses norm $-1$; odd-prime obstructions use an integral trace strictly between successive integers. The irrationality and trace-integrality requirements are explicit.

`QuadraticUnits.lean` constructs $J$ as the quotient by $X^2+1$. Irreducibility follows from the real embedding of $K$. The power basis establishes $[J:K]=2$, unique coordinates $a+bi$, and the conjugation fixing $K$ and sending $i$ to $-i$.

For odd primes, the norm of $\pm iu$ is $u^2$, so a prime root would contradict the existing $u^2$ obstruction. ## 4. Conjugate-factor descent

`ConjugateDescent.irreducible_of_swapped_factors` assumes an exact mapped product, two coprime irreducible factors, and a coefficient homomorphism fixing the embedded base field and carrying the first factor to the second. Those assumptions suffice: a base-field divisor containing one factor must contain its conjugate, and hence their product. Cancellation then makes the remaining base-field factor a unit.

The proof correctly handles nonzeroness, nonunitness, prime divisibility, coprimality, and descent of divisibility through an injective field map. It does not need to assume that the base is the entire fixed field or that the homomorphism is an involution, although the concrete consumers have genuine involutions. `coprime_linear_combinations` supplies an exact Bezout certificate using $(d-c)^{-1}$.

Over $J/K$, the two binomials $Y^m-iu$ and $Y^m+iu$ are coprime, irreducible, and exchanged by conjugation. Their product is $Y^{2m}+u^2$. Together with the odd case, this proves irreducibility of $Y^k+u^2$ over $K$ for every positive $k$.

## 5. Exact factors and both all-index irreducibility proofs

`EvenFactors.lean` defines the factors independently by their two initial values and polynomial recurrence. Their closed forms are proved for every index from that recurrence. Multiplying them yields the exact identity $P_{2k}=U_kV_k$ through the existing closed form for $P_N$; no factorization is assumed in defining either family.

The reviewer checked the signs and scalar normalizations:

$$
(u-v)U_k=-v\big((X+u)^k+u^2(X+v)^k\big),
\qquad
V_k=-v\big((X+u)^k-u^2(X+v)^k\big).
$$

The reused fractional-linear irreducibility lemma checks its exceptional denominator and leading-coefficient conditions. It proves $U_k$ irreducible over $K$ for every $k>0$, and $V_k$ irreducible over $K$ when $k$ is odd. Those results descend to $\mathbb Q$.

For $k=2m$, the Lucas factor splits over $K$ into

$$
q=(X+u)^m-u(X+v)^m,
\qquad
r=-v\big((X+u)^m+u(X+v)^m\big).
$$

Each factor is irreducible by the binomials with constants $\pm u$. Their coprimality is proved. The golden-field conjugation sends $q$ to **exactly** $r$ because $uv=-1$; the scalar $-v$ is correctly included. Descent proves $V_{2m}$ irreducible over $\mathbb Q$. No incorrect stronger claim of its irreducibility over $K$ is made.

The parity cases cover every positive half-degree. The degree proofs use nonvanishing leading coefficients of the transformed expressions and injectivity of the rational coefficient map, obtaining degree exactly $k$ for both factors. `factors_coprime_over_K`, and its rational descent in `EvenDerivative.lean`, make the two factors nonassociate for $k>0$; their product is therefore exactly the pair of distinct irreducible factors, up to units.

## 6. Original derivative and no-cancellation bridge

`DerivativePolynomials.poly_coprime_D` proves no cancellation at **every** positive degree. In the quadratic field, the only denominator roots are $-u,-v$. The proved closed form evaluates to nonzero values at both roots. The proof converts these evaluations to coprimality with each linear factor and then with their product. Coprimality descends to $\mathbb Q$.

The general `num_div_of_coprime` theorem uses the actual `RatFunc.num` definition and normalized polynomial gcd. It computes the scalar required to make the denominator monic. Specializing it gives the exact canonical numerator

$$
\operatorname{num}(f^{(n)})
=(-1)^{-(n+1)}n!P_{n+1}.
$$

For $n=2k-1$, the sign is positive and the endpoint is precisely

$$
\operatorname{num}(f^{(2k-1)})=(2k-1)!U_kV_k.
$$

`A326925Even.odd_derivative_numerator_factorization` includes the factorial in the first factor and proves exact equality, irreducibility of both factors, coprimality, and both degrees. The nonzero rational factorial is handled as a unit. The endpoint has no unproved source-agreement, denominator, or irreducibility assumption.
