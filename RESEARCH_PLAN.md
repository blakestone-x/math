# Open problems and next proof attempts

The first queue named eight problems. The continuation proves the two next line-graph maxima, lowers the binary stopping-time bound, establishes oscillation in every Gray residue class modulo 140, bounds Sigrist's partial sums, eliminates three divisor exponent classes, proves all-scale Hankel gcd bounds, establishes Franel irreducibility on 128 dyadic families, and reaches the Fibonacci growth rate for ordinary queen denominators. The manuscripts and precise hypotheses appear in the [scoreboard](SCOREBOARD.md).

## Next targets

| Priority | Question and current result | First proof attempt | Decisive outcome |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Is the largest line-graph signature at ten independent cycles 5 or 6? The conjecture predicts 5. [Eight- and nine-cycle proof](Line-graph-signature/eight-nine.md) | Start from a hypothetical signature 6 and bound its allowable deletions, contractions and signed endpoint equations. Enumerate only the surviving cubic kernels. The general finite reduction also gives a 209-vertex bound on an attaining root graph. | An explicit graph with signature 6 refutes the conjecture. An exhaustive obstruction at 6 proves the ten-cycle case. |
| 2 | Does Sun's Franel polynomial remain irreducible two or three steps after a dyadic index? For each certified odd base, the only possible eventual complementary factor in these two families is linear. [Newton polygon theorem](A000172/dyadic.pdf) | Apply the rational-root theorem after removing the polynomial content. Use reductions at different primes to constrain the constant and leading coefficients of that single factor. Begin with base 1, then extend a successful argument to other odd bases. | Exclude rational roots along an entire new family, or give an exact rational root in a conjectured case. |
| 3 | Can a three-prime fixed point of the weighted proper-divisor sum have smallest exponent 4? Exponents 1, 2, 3 and 5 are excluded. [Exponent bounds and verifiers](A245211/simple-prime.md) | At $c=4$ there are only $4\le b\le47$ and $b\le a\le16b+12$. The 22-modulus sieve reduces the 17,402 exponent pairs to 502. Bound the three prime bases for those 502 profiles, starting with the smallest surviving middle exponent. [Reproduce the sieve](A245211/simple-prime.md) | Rule out $c=4$ for arbitrary primes, or produce a fixed point distinct from 21. |
| 4 | Does every ordinary queen denominator through $F_q$ occur on $q$ labels? The exponential growth rate is sharp, and the maximum is exactly $F_q$ when new points come from successive intersections. [Interval and subclass theorem](Queen-denominator-interval/README.md) | For the full interval question, try to encode a prescribed denominator with the same label count as its Fibonacci expansion. For the maximum question, identify a reducible pair of attack equations in unrestricted vertices, or construct a counterexample to the bound. | Extend the interval to a fixed positive fraction of $F_q$ for all sufficiently large $q$, reach the exact endpoint without gaps, or settle the unrestricted maximum. |
| 5 | Are the two original Gray–Fibonacci ratio limit sets infinite? Each already has at least 280 accumulation points, with oscillation in every class modulo 140. [Both manuscripts and prefix calculations](A265387/universal.md) | Look for an inductive split that survives increasing prefix precision. The current proof separates the 140 phases and proves a return gap inside each one, but it supplies no split that repeats indefinitely. | Prove infinitely many distinct limits, or identify a finite set and prove that every subsequential limit belongs to it. |
| 6 | Does Sigrist's greedy binary-substring rule reach every positive integer? The relative jumps exceed any fixed positive threshold only on a set of upper Banach density zero. [Growth and range theorem](A316994/growth.md) | A recurring positive relative jump forces infinitely many limiting binary prefixes. Try to turn this into a contradiction for any fixed omitted word, or prove $a_{n+1}/S_n\to0$. The new prefix-crossing lemma makes the latter sufficient. | Prove the full permutation statement, or a uniform range bound of order $X^\delta$ for some $\delta>0$. |
| 7 | What is the sharp finite Hankel-word length modulo 8? For moduli 2 through 7 the sharp maxima are known. For Shallit's separate fixed infinite word, individual nonvanishing remains open. [Modular and fixed-word manuscripts](Shallit-Hankel/README.md) | For modulus 8, organize extensions by reduction modulo 4 and track determinants that are zero modulo 4 but survive modulo 8. For the fixed word, seek a closed recurrence at doubled order with a nonvanishing invariant. | Complete the modulus-8 tree with a certified upper bound and attaining word, or prove an infinite class of individual determinants is nonzero at every shift. |
| 8 | Is the worst binary reverse-subtraction stopping time subexponential in the half-width $m$? Its certified upper bound is $O(58^{m/20})$. [Shell proof](A338203/shells.pdf) · [Two independent verifiers](A338203/README.md) | Bound the number of marked self-loops on a route through a width-$k$ prefix graph as a function of $k$. A subexponential bound in $k$ would imply a subexponential stopping time. Beginning at marked vertices cuts the search to paths that can contribute to this bound. | Prove an upper bound of the form $e^{o(m)}$, or construct a family with provably superlinear stopping time. |
| 9 | Do the rows of the Eulerian triangle square have only real roots? This is the other clause of Mao and Wang's Conjecture 4.1. [Delannoy proof and source](Delannoy-square/README.md) | Test whether the row-transform iteration preserves strict interlacing in a suitable interval. An analogous interval-preservation theorem proves the Delannoy clause. Track the Eulerian recurrence separately. | Prove the Eulerian clause in all degrees, an infinite family of degrees, or an exact counterexample. |

## Verification and publication

1. Record the original statement, its hypotheses, index conventions, known cases and literature. Identify what a new proof would add.
2. Assign an independent reader or an alternative computation to the critical step. For an exhaustive proof, publish the finite reduction, coverage argument, code and expected output. Give the exact theorem checked by Lean when a formalization is available.
3. Upload the editable manuscript and PDF together. Link the result, remaining open question and verification method from the scoreboard. Credit concurrent project changes when updating the research index.
